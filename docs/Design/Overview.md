## Age Progression system

The Waning Border is not like other traditional RTS games. Every player starts
on equal footing — there are only **two "ages"**.

- **Age 0** — All players have the same stats and the same options.
  Players must read the map and their enemies in order to plan for their next
  steps. See [Age_0.md](Age_0.md) for the full buildings / units / tech list.
  Every player's capital is the **Shelter** (id `Fortress`); there is no Hall,
  no King's Court and no Town Hall.

- **Age 1** — Players choose one of three cultures:

  | Culture | Focus |
  |---------|-------|
  | **Feraldis** | Military |
  | **Runai** | Economy |
  | **Alanthor** | Defense |

  At age-up the Shelter becomes the **Fortress** automatically, for every
  culture. From age-up onward, all progression is made through **building
  upgrades** (up to **level 3**; each culture's level 1 is free at age-up, and
  later level prices live on the building level SOs) and **faction-wide
  technologies**. There is no Age 2 / Age 3 / Imperial — the depth of the
  game lives in the culture × sect × upgrade matrix, not in further ages.

## North-star: the Movement axis

Every faction's supply mechanic, military identity, and territorial behavior
is a different **relationship to movement on the map**. This is the single
clearest axis the game rotates around — when adding any new unit, building,
or tech, the first design question is *"how does this interact with
movement?"*.

| Faction | Relationship to movement | Economy is generated by… |
|---------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Alanthor** | **Denies** movement | …holding ground — income is the territory it owns and what it builds on it ([Territory_Claims.md](Territory_Claims.md), [Veilstone_Economy.md](Veilstone_Economy.md)); walls and towers are how it keeps that ground. *(The old "closed wall compartments yield supplies" income was removed 2026-07-06; workers no longer gather.)* |
| **Feraldis** | **Preys on** movement | …inflicting damage (enemy / Border kills generate supplies) |
| **Runai** | **Embodies** movement | …moving (caravan-wagons in transit + lane-patrolling traders) |

Three corollary rules fall out of this axis:

1. **Every faction's supply mechanic needs a floor** — a thing the player
   can do alone, in their own territory, if the map isn't cooperating.
   Alanthor's floor is fortifying and developing the ground it already holds;
   Feraldis's floor is **Veilstone
   Border creatures / nodes count as damage targets** (so an isolated
   Feraldis player can always farm income from the border); Runai's floor is
   their patrolling trader-warriors generating supplies along their own
   lanes regardless of who they meet.
2. **Asymmetric matchups are built into the triangle**: Feraldis preys on
   movement, Runai depends on movement, Alanthor denies movement. A
   Feraldis-vs-Runai match is a chase economy; a Feraldis-vs-Alanthor match
   is a siege economy; a Runai-vs-Alanthor match is a perimeter economy.
   These dynamics emerge for free — don't script them.
3. **Resist erosion of identity-defining absences.** Runai has **no
   walls** and **no Houses** (per the trade-lane design pass). Feraldis
   gets **the full game population cap at age-up** (no Houses needed for pop), but
   *does* have Houses as a pure **raider-spawn / aggression tool** —
   each House build / upgrade spawns autonomous Raiders that attack
   the closest enemy. These absences and twists do real design work
   (lanes-as-defense for Runai, housing-as-pressure for Feraldis). Don't
   hand out "just a small palisade" to Runai or "Houses without raiders"
   to Feraldis later — each concession dilutes the triangle.

### The influence map (decided 2026-07-06)

> **SUPERSEDED.** Influence maps were removed on 2026-08-31
> ([Regions.md](Regions.md) §3b): territories have fixed shapes and ownership
> is decided by [Territory_Claims.md](Territory_Claims.md) (the ownership
> meter, build only on owned ground). The section below is kept as history; do
> not implement from it. The blood map paragraph at its end still describes a
> live grid.

Territory is tracked on a shared **influence map** with **one channel per
player (8) plus curse** — a cell with no channel above the display threshold
reads as **neutral**. Rules:

- The whole map starts at **0 influence** (all neutral).
- **Age 0 buildings and units grant no influence** — territory only becomes
  a factor once cultures (or the curse) start pushing.
- **Curse units and buildings** (the Border's nodes and creatures) **slowly
  spread curse influence** around themselves; it fades back to neutral when
  the source is destroyed.
- **Age 1 Alanthor buildings grant their owner's influence** (2026-07-06
  extension): the **Fortress (the capital) is a potent source** (a fresh Age 1 start
  must be able to build within seconds — it owns nothing but its Fortress);
  fortifications (wall hubs, wall segments, watch towers) claim fast;
  **every other Alanthor building produces much less** influence;
  **Gatherer's Huts grant none**. This replaces the removed wall-compartment
  supply income as Alanthor's territorial identity.
- **Runai and Feraldis grant influence the same way** (2026-07-06 rev.4):
  every cultured faction's Fortress is the potent anchor and its other buildings
  grant the weak civic rate. Signature sources on top: **Runai trade nodes**
  claim strongly and the **lanes between same-faction trade nodes** deposit
  a corridor of influence; **Feraldis totem towers** stake strong claims.
- **Influence decays back to neutral once its source is gone** —
  proportional decay (5 %/s): the border collapses in ~15 s, the last
  visible trace fades within ~45 s. Sources continuously outpace decay to
  hold ground. The simulation ticks at 0.1 s with correspondingly small
  per-tick deposits, so growth and collapse read smoothly.
- **Feraldis is the exception: its influence never decays** (2026-08-18).
  Ground a Feraldis player has claimed stays claimed for the rest of the
  match even with every source dead — it can only be **replaced**. A
  Feraldis cell loses strength only while **another player's channel or the
  curse** sits at or above it on that cell, and it then erodes at the
  normal decay rate until it is gone (values saturate at the same ceiling,
  so matching a saturated cell is enough to start taking it). Feraldis
  claims by walking on you; what it has bled for, it keeps until someone
  takes it off them. See
  [Age_1_Feraldis.md § Feraldis influence never decays](Age_1_Feraldis.md#feraldis-influence-never-decays).
- **The border is the 0.5 iso-contour** of a channel. Influence **outside a
  border is invisible** — only the ≥0.5 interior renders, outlined by a
  continuous border line that conforms to the territory shape.
- **Alanthor players cannot build outside their own influence** — placement
  requires their channel ≥ 0.5 at the build position (walls included: the
  border grows one influence-covered hop at a time). **Exempt: Gatherer's
  Huts** (grant no influence, may harvest anywhere) **and Watch Towers**
  (forward claims — they project influence into new ground). *(Today: every
  culture builds only on owned ground, [Territory_Claims.md](Territory_Claims.md).)*
- **Economy inside the border was boosted** (wired to the retired Gatherer's
  Hut area income).
- **Guild Surveys** (Alanthor-only faction-wide research at the Guild, the
  cultured Gatherer's Hut): Guild huts also produce Iron / Veilstone /
  Veilsteel — a deliberate exception to the veilstone rules, see
  [Age_1_Alanthor.md](Age_1_Alanthor.md) and
  [Veilstone_Economy.md](Veilstone_Economy.md). (Deep Gathering REMOVED
  2026-08-04.)
- The influence map is **covered by fog of war** (2026-07-06 rev.4 — it is
  NOT public information): unexplored ground shows no influence anywhere;
  explored-but-unseen ground shows it dimmed, like the terrain beneath it.
- Display: **neutral = no colour, curse = purple, players = their player
  colour**. Minimap = tinted fill + border; in-world overlay = **border
  lines only**, traced as smooth splines along the 0.5 contour. Claimed
  ground can also repaint the terrain via layers named `AlanthorInfluence` /
  `RunaiiInfluence` / `FeraldisInfluence` / `CurseInfluence`.
- **The blood map** is a separate, independent grid: every non-Border unit
  death spills blood at its position (scaled by max HP). Blood fades over
  **about three minutes** — far slower than influence — and can
  repaint the terrain via a layer named `Blood`. It is the substrate for
  Feraldis blood mechanics ([Fire.md](Fire.md) §4). Displayed like influence: a dark-red
  stain on the minimap and a dark-red contour spline (level 0.35) in the
  world overlay, both fog-of-war gated.

### Population model (cross-faction summary)

The rules; the numbers (pop per House level, pop the capital provides, each
unit's pop cost) are on the SOs. The game-wide hard cap is
`FactionPopulation.AbsoluteMax` in code.

| Faction | Pop source | Pop cap behavior |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Age 0 (everyone)** | The Shelter plus Houses (id `Hut`) | The opening cap is the Shelter's pop plus one House's. |
| **Alanthor** | Houses (pop rises with each House level) + the Fortress | Gradual ramp via building investment. |
| **Runai** | **The full game cap at age-up** — no House exists for Runai | Unlocks all at once on culture pick. |
| **Feraldis** | **The full game cap at age-up** — no House required for pop | Unlocks all at once on culture pick. Houses still exist but only spawn Raiders; they do not contribute pop. |

## Age-up: transform, don't replace

Age-up should feel like a **power spike, not a reset.** The pre-culture
**Gatherer's Hut** is the common root of all three economies — *raiding* is
"violent gathering," *walling* is "protected gathering," *trading* is
"networked gathering." Each faction's age-up transformation grows out of the
huts the player already invested in, so the player's Age 0 placement
decisions stay relevant.

| Faction | Hut transforms into… | Immediate Age-1 power-spike |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Alanthor** | The **Guild** — each Gatherer's Hut keeps its place and its supply income and gains the Guild's level ladder and research (Surveys, reinforcement). *(The per-hut Wall Hub / Watch Tower conversion prompt was retired 2026-07-08; stone walls and Watch Towers are built directly — see [Age_1_Alanthor.md](Age_1_Alanthor.md).)* | Every Guild arrives at level 1 for free at age-up, and the Guild Surveys let Alanthor huts produce iron, veilstone and veilsteel — the only huts in the game that do |
| **Feraldis** | A **raider unit** that auto-patrols outward seeking targets | Roaming raiders that start generating damage-income immediately |
| **Runai** | A **mobile caravan-wagon** the player deploys outward to plant the first trade post | Wagons output **full income while in transit** — age-up is Runai's peak income moment |

These transformations are the *only* free-territory bursts each faction
gets. Every trade post / wall / raid party built after age-up costs workers
+ resources at the normal rate. Lose a transformation (e.g. a Runai wagon
killed in transit) and the player loses both:

- **Material cost** — equivalent to one Gatherer's Hut.
- **Tempo cost** — they must now establish that piece of map the slow way
  (send a worker, build a wall, recruit a unit manually).

> **Playtest heuristic** for tuning Feraldis vs Runai aggression: in a 1v1,
> the Runai player should land roughly **70–80 %** of their age-up wagons
> at destination with reasonable escort. Lower than that, Feraldis is too
> punishing on the transition; near 100 %, escorting doesn't matter and
> the mechanic becomes decorative.

Per-faction mechanics and behaviors are in [Age_1_Alanthor.md](Age_1_Alanthor.md), [Age_1_Feraldis.md](Age_1_Feraldis.md), and [Age_1_Runai.md](Age_1_Runai.md); costs are on the SOs.

## Unit granularity — single units vs battalions

The Waning Border mixes two unit-control models. **Which model a unit uses
is determined by its category, not by faction:**

| Category | Granularity | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Economy** | **Single entity** per unit | Worker, Scout |
| **Player-trained military** | **Battalion** of N soldiers (predefined size — some sizes still TBD) controlled as one | Spearman, Swordsman, Sentinel, Archer, Crossbowman, Berserker, Hunter, Skirmisher, … |
| **Heroes** | **Single entity** per unit | King Lexor; every sect unit is a hero ([Religion.md](Religion.md) §4, [Heroes.md](Heroes.md)) |
| **Building-spawned units** | **Single entity** per unit. **Usually uncontrolled** by the player (auto-patrol / auto-trade / auto-fight). | Runai Caravan, Runai Escort, Runai Trader-Warrior ⚠, Feraldis hut-raider ⚠ |

Stat values for battalion units live on their SOs. Whether an SO's stat is
the battalion's pooled value or a per-soldier value needs an explicit
verification pass once the battalion sizes are finalized.

## Military upgrades are faction-wide (cross-faction rule)

> **Decided 2026-10-03 (Unification decision 31).** The earlier per-battalion
> upgrade model (research unlocks an "Upgrade" button on each battalion, paid
> per battalion) is **DROPPED**. Links elsewhere that still say
> "per-battalion" point here and mean this rule.

Researching a weapon-, arrow-, armour- or tool-tier tech applies it to the
whole faction at once: every existing unit of the classes it covers is
upgraded on completion, and every unit trained afterwards comes out at that
tier. There is no per-battalion upgrade button and no per-battalion price.

The ladders are Stone → Iron → Veilstone → Shard-infused (weapons; the
equivalent arrow, armour and tool ladders follow the same shape). Each tier's
host building, building-level gate (the tech SO's `minBuildingLevel`, the only
level gate) and price are on its tech SO.

## Resource carry — Veilsteel Frenzy (Feraldis-only)

Earlier code defined two cross-faction "carry resource → unit attack bonus"
loops (`ironCarry` and `veilsteel.carrySlots`).

These cross-faction loops are **retired.** In the design:

- **Iron-carry is removed entirely.** No unit of any faction gets an
  attack bonus from carrying Iron.
- **Veilsteel-carry is Feraldis-only**, gated behind the **Veilsteel
  Frenzy** tech (the renamed `Feraldis_IronFury`), researched at the
  Feraldis War Hall. Feraldis units carry up to 5 Veilsteel shavings;
  each shaving grants +2 % attack (stacks to +10 %).

> **Why the rewrite:** to give Feraldis a distinct, lore-rich combat-
> economy loop ("consume Veilsteel shavings as a psychoactive battle
> stimulant", evoking Norse berserkers and mushrooms) rather than a flat
> cross-faction bonus. Alanthor and Runai derive their attack edge from
> walls / faction-wide tech ladders and lane synergy respectively — they don't
> need an additional generic carry-bonus.

## Map resources — Veilstone and Veilsteel (directive 2026-07-04)

> **SUPERSEDED by [Veilstone_Economy.md](Veilstone_Economy.md) (2026-10-01).**
> Veilstone comes from outcrops (Inactive / Cursed / Depleted) and the curse
> refills depleted ones; **veilsteel is made, never mined** — there are no
> veilsteel nodes. Kept as history.

- **Veilstone behaves exactly like Iron.** Fixed patches seeded at map
  start, mined until depleted, gone forever. The former respawn economy
  (Border deaths reverting to a "potential veilstone" pool, per-minute
  distribution, patch regrowth, excess-spawned new clusters) is
  **stripped entirely**.
- **Veilsteel was also a map resource: the "Veilsteel Mine" node** (called
  "Sharp Crystals" in older docs and in the internal `VeilsteelDeposit*`
  identifiers), a single node placed per map via a `VeilsteelDepositMarker`.

## High-ground rule — ranged units (directive 2026-07-04)

Ranged units are affected by the height difference between shooter and
target, **calculated per shot**:

- Shooting a target **below** you: **more range and more damage**.
- Shooting a target **above** you: **less range and less damage**.
- One shared multiplier drives both: **4 % per meter** of height
  difference, capped at **±20 %** (full effect at 5 m). These are the rule's
  constants (`PerMeter`, `MaxEffect` in `HeightAdvantage.cs`), not unit stats.

Applies to every ranged unit — the whole ArcherState family (Archer,
Crossbowman, Alanthor Crossbowman, Longbowman, Ballista) **and** the
Border's Veilstinger (each of its two guns scales against its own
target's elevation). Melee is unaffected. Implementation:
`Systems/Combat/Attacks/HeightAdvantage.cs`.

## Religious units — cross-faction "game-ender" tier

The culture-specific religious units (Runai's **Acolyte**, Feraldis's
**Iconoclast**; Alanthor's Holy Scholar is cut, 2026-10-03) are unified
into a **single "game-ender" cost bracket**. They are intentionally:

- **Tanky**
- **Slow**
- **Single units**, not battalions
- **Hard to replace** (long training time, high resource cost)
- **Strategically decisive** — each unblocks its faction's interaction with
  the curse

Their stats and prices are on their SOs; the bracket is the design intent the
SOs are priced to.

| Unit | Train at |
|------|----------|
| **Acolyte** (Runai) | Temple of Ridan |
| **Iconoclast** (Feraldis) | Temple of Ridan |

> **No Temple levels** (2026-10-03): the religious units train at the
> Temple of Ridan, which is never upgraded (one per faction).

> **No Glow in the cost.** Iconoclast was the unit that *unblocked* Glow
> generation for Feraldis (by stripping `NodeUntargetable` from border
> nodes). Requiring Glow to train any religious unit would have been a chicken-
> and-egg. Losing one of these units is meant to be a major setback.
> (The Glow economy itself is superseded — see below.)

## Caravan kills feed Feraldis (cross-faction synergy)

When a Runai **caravan** is killed, the 50 % cargo drop is awarded
**only** to the killer if the killer is **Feraldis** (the cargo converts
to supplies for the Feraldis player). Alanthor and Runai killers gain
nothing — the cargo is destroyed.

| Killer | Cargo outcome |
|--------|---------------|
| Feraldis unit | **50 % of cargo → killer's supplies** |
| Alanthor unit | destroyed |
| Runai unit (friendly-fire / PvE) | destroyed |
| Border creature | destroyed |

This is a deliberate **asymmetric synergy with Feraldis's damage-as-income
economy**: their kill-economy mechanic already rewards killing
non-military units (via Pillage), and caravan kills feed the same loop
with bigger payouts. Runai players will need to escort caravans or
re-route them around known Feraldis patrol zones.

> **Cross-link:** see [Age_1_Feraldis.md § Pillage](Age_1_Feraldis.md#culture-identity) (the Pillage tech scope) and [Age_1_Runai.md § Caravan](Age_1_Runai.md#runai-caravan--civilian-trade-uncontrollable) (caravan death rules).

## Border neutrality (Runai-only)

Runai gains **tech-gated neutrality** to the The Border over time.
Researched at the Trader's Hall. Example tech shape: −20 % chance of
aggroing border waves when Runai units traverse border tiles. Border
defences themselves stay in place — the border resists *conversion*
(Runai's interaction type) harder than it resists *cleansing* (Alanthor)
or *destruction* (Feraldis).

Detail: [Age_1_Runai.md § Trader's Hall — researchable techs](Age_1_Runai.md#researchable-techs).

## Border node lifecycle — destruction & rebuild (directive 2026-07)

> **SUPERSEDED by [Territory_Claims.md](Territory_Claims.md) §6 (2026-09-29).**
> Curse nodes are destructible structures the curse builds on resource nodes;
> there are no wells, verbs or Purification. Kept as history.

A **Border main node** was destroyable by ordinary combat — any faction
could bring an **Active** node to 0 HP. Destruction was **temporary**; only an
**Alanthor Purification permanently removed a node**.

| State | Trigger | Behaviour |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| **Active** | default / rebuild complete | Spawns waves, self-defends, targetable. |
| **Destroyed — rubble** | HP reaches 0 | Inert, untargetable. Leaves a rubble pile for a while. |
| **Destroyed — rebuilding** | the rubble period ends | Reconstructs, still inert/untargetable. |
| **Cleansed** | Alanthor Purification ritual | **Permanent.** Inert husk — no waves, no turret, never reverts. |
| **Converted** | Runai Conversion ritual | Runai infrastructure; reverts to Active over time (unchanged). |

- **Destroy → rubble → auto-rebuild:** a destroyed node lay as rubble, then
  rebuilt and returned to Active. The node was untargetable and harmless the
  whole time.
- **Purification was the only permanent answer:** Alanthor Purification could
  cleanse a node whether it was Active **or** lying in rubble / rebuilding.
- **Feraldis instant-win window:** because a destroyed node stayed in the
  Destroyed state for the full rubble+rebuild span, Feraldis could
  chain-destroy every node inside that window for the instant victory.
- **Iconoclast note:** with nodes generally destroyable, the old
  Iconoclast "only unit that can destroy a node" gate is retired; the aura
  targetability system was replaced by state-based targetability.

## The Glow economy (cross-faction)

> **SUPERSEDED** by [Curse_And_Shardroot.md](Curse_And_Shardroot.md),
> [Territory_Claims.md](Territory_Claims.md) and
> [Veilstone_Economy.md](Veilstone_Economy.md). There is no Glow resource:
> the top tier of every weapon / arrow / armour ladder is **Shard-infused**,
> priced in veilstone and veilsteel on its tech SO. Kept as history.

**Glow** was a high-tier resource, **only created** by interacting with
**Border nodes**, each node yielding Glow **once** — the first time its
state changed — so back-and-forth contesting could not fabricate infinite
Glow. Sources were the first cleanse (Alanthor), conversion (Runai) or
destruction (Feraldis) of a node; it dropped as a ground pickup, could be
looted off a Glow-carrying unit or building, and gated the T4 tier of every
ladder, which made Glow supply finite per map.

## The Petriarchy System

In the world of The Waning Border, **12 religious sects** control different
aspects of life and rulership.

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-29 by [Religion.md](Religion.md):** RP come from
> killing curse units (or the Temple's Tithe), the Temple is an Age 0 building
> bought with RP, chapels are bought with RP (cheaper with affinity), sect
> units are heroes recruited at their chapel, and double chapels and
> Temple-driven sect levels are retired. Kept for the Temple and chapel
> architecture.

Build the Temple of Ridan (Age 0, one per faction). Out of the 12 sects,
**6 can be chosen** — one per chapel site. Each adopted sect unlocks:

- Its **active powers** (levels bought with RP, [Religion.md](Religion.md) §3.1)
- One **passive power**
- One **unit** — a hero ([Religion.md](Religion.md) §4)
- Its **chapel** (the sect's building slot)
- One **technology**

Different sects have different synergies with your specific culture and with
each other, and you have a **limited amount of religion points** to spend on
adopting or upgrading sects. Choose wisely.

**Playable-sect rollout (decided 2026-07-05).** Only sects with a fully
implemented lever kit can be adopted: currently **Justice, Renewal, and
War**. The other nine show "(Coming soon)" in every adoption UI and are
refused authoritatively (AI included). Each adopted sect's unit is recruited
**at its chapel** ([Religion.md](Religion.md) §4). The gate lives in
`SectConfig.IsImplemented` — widen it as sects land.

**Temple architecture & chapel rework (2026-08-02).** The Temple of Ridan is
a **seven-sided cathedral**: one face carries a **large door** (the temple
entrance); the **other six faces each host a chapel site**, so a fully
diversified late game runs **six different sects** at once. The cathedral is
the **Temple's** look (the Temple has no levels, 2026-10-03). Its footprint
matches the heptagon (cells in [Build_Grid.md](Build_Grid.md)); chapels dock
**flush against their temple face like BFME2 fortress expansions** (back wall
against the temple, door facing outward). Visually the
temple **mixes all three cultures** — Alanthor white-slate walls, Runai
sandstone bands and door frame, Feraldis iron corner pylons and roof — and
is crowned by a **tall central pinnacle** with a glowing tip. Chapels are
styled by their sect's **affinity culture**, each with a unique,
instantly-identifiable roof:

**Chapels are SECT STATUES (2026-08-02)** — monuments docked to the temple
faces, each a figure in its cluster's stone holding the sect's tool.
**White marble** for Alanthor, **red sandstone** for Runaii, **obsidian**
for Feraldis.

| Chapel statue (cluster) | Figure & tool |
|-------------------------|---------------|
| Renewal (Alanthor) | Man in robes raising a crystal-blue cup |
| Antiquity (Alanthor) | Bearded scholar reading a stone tablet |
| Justice (Runaii) | Soldier holding out a golden noose |
| Silence (Runaii) | Veiled figure cradling a dim orb |
| War (Feraldis) | Armored soldier holding a severed enemy head |
| Ash (Feraldis) | Hooded mourner bearing an ember-lit urn |

*(The other six sects reuse their cluster-pair's statue until they get
their own.)*

> **Retired:** double chapels (a second chapel of an adopted sect) and the
> Temple-driven sect progression (sects levelling with Temple levels, per-age
> RP awards). The Temple has no levels and chapel levels are bought with RP
> ([Religion.md](Religion.md) §3). Each sect's power kit is in

**Sect powers wind up (decided 2026-07-05, tiers 2026-09-27).** Every power
telegraphs its circle on the ground before the effect lands, so the target can
dodge out and offensive casts are counterable: **3 s** for a power that deals
damage, **1.5 s** for a hostile power that does not, **1 s** for a friendly
one ([Spells.md](Spells.md) §4 and §8.6). The cooldown starts at cast, not at
impact.
